Leaving the Netherlands: a checklist for internationals

By Olav and Rosalie Gubbels · Updated 18 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • Start with the apartment. Notice, handover instructions and clearance take the most time.
  • Tell your municipality you are leaving. The exact steps differ per municipality.
  • Keep a Dutch bank account open for a few weeks after you leave, for refunds and your deposit.
  • Take photos and meter readings on your last day.

Start with the apartment

Most people begin with flights and shipping. The rental is the part that goes wrong more often, because it has a hard deadline: the day you hand back the keys. Start here.

  • Read your contract. Look for the notice period and any clauses about the state you must leave the home in.
  • Give notice in writing and ask your landlord to confirm the end date.
  • Ask for the handover instructions in writing. Many landlords and agents have a list of what they expect: empty, cleaned, walls repaired, flooring removed or left. Work from that list, not from guesses.
  • Book a pre-inspection if your landlord offers one. It shows you what still needs doing while there is time to do it.

Six to eight weeks before you leave

  • Decide what travels with you, what you sell, what you donate and what goes. Our guide on what to do with your furniture helps with this.
  • List every subscription with a notice period: internet, gym, phone, insurance, parking permit.
  • Tell your employer or HR that you are leaving, so payroll and tax are handled in time.
  • Request quotes for clearance and cleaning. Dates around the end of the month fill up first.

Two to four weeks before

  • Start selling furniture. Items that sell quickly are the ones that are easy to collect.
  • Report your departure to your municipality. Amsterdam publishes the steps on amsterdam.nl, and the rules differ per municipality, so check the current process there.
  • If you have a residence permit and are leaving for good, find out how to return it to the IND. The ACCESS guide to deregistration explains the options.
  • Ask your health insurer what you need to do. Do not cancel before you have cover elsewhere, and stop any allowances you receive, such as healthcare or rent benefit.
  • Arrange mail forwarding to your new address.

The last week

  • Clear the apartment and clean it. If you hire someone, agree the date and the price before they start.
  • Read the electricity, gas and water meters. Take a photo of each, with the date visible.
  • Photograph every room once it is empty, including inside cupboards and the storage room.
  • Hand over the keys. Count them, and ask for the signed handover report on the spot.

After you have left

  • Keep your Dutch bank account open for a few weeks. Your deposit, utility refunds and some other refunds arrive there. ACCESS notes that a refund for a cancelled parking permit can take up to six weeks.
  • Keep your DigiD active if you may need to claim back tax. It stops working after a period of non-use, and it is hard to get a new one from abroad.
  • Chase your deposit if it is late. Our guide on getting your deposit back lists the deadlines.
  • Ask about taxes. Whether you need a final Dutch tax return depends on your situation. We are not tax advisers, so ask your employer or an adviser.

What if you cannot be here for the last weeks?

This happens often: the new job starts before the lease ends. You can still hand back the apartment properly. Read moving out of Amsterdam when you are already abroad.
