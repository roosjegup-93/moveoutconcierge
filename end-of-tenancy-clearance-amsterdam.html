End-of-tenancy clearance in Amsterdam: what "empty" really means

By Olav and Rosalie Gubbels · Updated 18 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  • Unless you agreed otherwise, you hand the home back empty, not just tidy.
  • Your landlord's handover instructions decide what counts as finished. Ask for them in writing.
  • Anything you installed yourself, such as flooring, lamps or curtains, needs an agreement if it stays.
  • Price depends mostly on volume, stairs, access and how much has to be disposed of.

What "empty" means

When a tenancy ends, you give the home back in the state you received it, apart from normal wear and tear. In practice that means everything that is yours is gone: furniture, appliances you brought, boxes, bikes, things in the storage room, the balcony and the attic. It also means rubbish is gone, not just tucked away.

Some landlords want more than that. They may ask for walls to be repaired, holes filled or the floor cleaned in a certain way. That is why the first step is always the same: ask for the handover instructions in writing.

What usually has to go

  • All furniture and loose items that belong to you.
  • Things you installed yourself, unless the landlord or the next tenant agrees to take them over. This includes flooring, curtain rails, lights, shelves and shower fittings.
  • Items in the storage room, balcony, garden and any shared spaces.
  • Bikes. They are easy to forget and hard to explain to a landlord afterwards.
  • Rubbish and recycling, including paint pots and other items the municipality will not collect from the kerb.

Furnished or unfurnished

In an unfurnished home, everything you brought goes. In a furnished home, the landlord's items stay, and you compare the home with the inventory list from the start of the lease. Missing or damaged items are what get deducted. If you added things of your own, those still have to go.

When something is supposed to stay

Sometimes you leave a floor, a kitchen unit or a lamp behind on purpose. Get this in writing, with a date, and mention it in the handover report. A spoken "fine, leave it" is not much help if a different person does the inspection.

Doing it yourself or hiring help

A small studio with a few pieces of furniture is doable on your own, if you have a van, time and a place to take the items. It gets harder when:

  • the apartment is on an upper floor with a narrow staircase, and in older houses large pieces sometimes have to be lowered out through a window;
  • you are on a deadline, because disposal points close, need an appointment or are far away;
  • you are in another country, or work all day until the last week;
  • there is more than you expected once the cupboards are opened.

Get the details right

  • Plan access: where the van can stop, and whether the building has rules about moving hours or a lift booking.
  • Keep valuables, documents and anything personal out of the clearance. Put them in one room or one box before the team arrives.
  • After clearing, photograph each room. The photos are your proof if there is a discussion about the deposit.
